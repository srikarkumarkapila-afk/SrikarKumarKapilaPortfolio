Discovering more than academic success

In India, I had advanced a grade. When my family moved to Canada in 2019, age-based placement put me in fifth grade for two months, even though I had completed sixth grade. I felt discouraged, but the additional time gave me room to explore programming, robotics, and chess. These interests became an important part of my life.

Building habits through a demanding routine

After moving to the United States, I attended a magnet high school where I could take college courses. The opportunity came with two hours on the school bus each morning and another two each evening. Balancing that commute with my coursework taught me to manage my time carefully.

Adapting to another new beginning

Moving to Mason, Ohio, before my junior year brought another adjustment. Differences in how my honors coursework was recognized lowered my weighted GPA. I continued to challenge myself and take college courses. I graduated summa cum laude from William Mason High School in 2026, in the top 5% of my class, with 70 college credits.

Moving forward with support

Shortly before my first university tuition payment was due, I learned that a change in my student status had made me ineligible for the Ohio Governor’s Merit Scholarship. I remain grateful for my family’s support as I began this next stage of my education.

These experiences have broadened what achievement means to me. It includes finding new interests when plans change, building habits under difficult circumstances, and continuing to learn. These are the lessons I want to carry into engineering and the challenges ahead.
